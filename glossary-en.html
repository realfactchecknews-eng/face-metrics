Looksmax Glossary

Looksmaxxing, PSL culture and blackpill aesthetics — explained simply.

Looksmaxxing is the deliberate optimisation of your appearance. It splits into softmax (no surgery: skincare, grooming, style, body fat, posture) and hardmax (cosmetic procedures and surgery). The terms below are exactly what the FaceRate report uses. All of this is an aesthetic framework, not a verdict.

Core concepts

PSL
A 10-point facial rating scale named after three forums (PerfectSkull / Lookism / Looksmax). A PSL score is a “hard”, no-flattery rating where 10 is a model-tier ideal.
Looksmaxxing
The full set of actions taken to maximise attractiveness — from skincare to surgery.
Softmax / Hardmax
Softmax — improvements without surgery (haircut, beard, skin, weight, posture, photo angles). Hardmax — procedures (implants, rhinoplasty, orthognathic surgery, fillers).
Mewing
Keeping the tongue flat against the palate at all times. Proponents claim it supports maxillary development and sharpens the jawline. Scientifically debated for adults, but a foundational term in the culture.
Mogging / to mog
To visibly outclass someone in looks. “He mogs him” = looks clearly better side by side.
Halo effect
One strong feature (e.g. eyes) makes the whole face read as more attractive.
Dimorphism
How strongly “masculine” traits (heavy jaw, brow ridge, cheekbones) or “feminine” traits (soft features, large eyes) are expressed.

Attractiveness tiers (PSL scale)

1–4 — below average
Noticeable aesthetic weaknesses pulling the face down the scale.
4–6.5 — the normie range
Ordinary looks, low-average to high-average. Most people live here.
7–8 — above average
An attractive, harmonious face with clear strong features.
8.5–10 — model tier
Rare, near-ideal looks by the canons of proportion and symmetry.
Pretty boy / Masculine
Two different routes to attractiveness: refined, soft features — or pronounced masculinity (heavy jaw, brows, cheekbones).

Eyes & periorbital area

Canthal tilt
The angle of the line between the inner and outer eye corners. Positive (outer corner higher) is considered attractive; negative (outer corner lower) reads “tired”. Neutral is horizontal.
Hunter eyes vs Prey eyes
Hunter eyes — deep-set, elongated, positive tilt with hooded lids (a dominant, “predatory” gaze). Prey eyes — round, wide open, with scleral show.
Eye hooding
How much the upper lid covers the eye. Slight hooding strengthens the hunter-eye look.
Scleral show
Visible white below/above the iris. Lower scleral show often ages the face and reads as “prey”.
Orbital rim
The bony rim of the eye socket. A strong, forward lower rim (“supportive”) signals a well-developed midface.
IPD
Interpupillary distance. Affects harmony; medium-wide spacing usually beats narrow.
PFL / PFH
Palpebral fissure length and height. Long, almond-shaped eyes rate above round ones.

Midface & cheekbones

Maxilla / maxillary projection
The upper jaw. Forward grown is the foundation of a good face: it supports the eyes, cheekbones and nose. Recessed hurts almost everything.
Zygomatic arch
The bony base of the cheekbones. High, prominent, wide-set cheekbones are a key attractiveness marker.
Malar / submalar
Malar — the “apple” of the cheek; the malar fat pad gives it volume. The submalar hollow beneath creates the “model shadow”.
Midface ratio
Midface length to width. A compact (short) midface usually looks better than a long one.
Ogee curve
The S-curve from cheek to cheekbone in profile/three-quarter view. A defined ogee = a full, youthful midface.

Jaw & chin

Jawline
The lower-jaw contour. A sharp, defined jawline is a top masculinity marker.
Gonial angle
The jaw angle at the gonion. Ideal ≈120–125°. Too obtuse looks soft; too acute looks harsh.
Ramus
The vertical branch of the mandible (from jaw angle to ear). Good ramus height gives a “deep”, masculine face.
Bigonial width
Width between the jaw angles. Drives how massive the lower third reads.
Chin projection
How far the chin projects forward. Good projection balances the profile; a weak chin drags the lower third down.
Submental angle
The angle between neck and chin. Sharp = clean jawline; obtuse (double chin, high body fat) blurs it.

Proportions & measurements

fWHR
Facial width-to-height ratio (bizygomatic width over midface height). Masculine range ≈1.9–2.1; linked to perceived dominance.
Facial thirds
Forehead / midface / lower third. The ideal is roughly equal thirds (33/33/33).
Golden ratio / 1.618
The classic proportion often cited as the aesthetic ideal of feature harmony.
Symmetry
How closely the left and right halves match. Mild asymmetry is normal for everyone; strong asymmetry visibly affects perception.
Face shapes
Oval (balanced), Square (strong jaw), Heart (wide forehead + narrow jaw), Diamond (prominent cheekbones), Oblong (elongated), Round (soft contour).

Nose & lips

Dorsum
The bridge line in profile: straight, convex (hump) or concave.
Nasal tip / projection
Tip projection and rotation; drives the nasolabial angle.
Alar width
Nostril width; ideally ≈ the intercanthal distance.
Vermillion / Cupid's bow
Vermillion — the red of the lips (fullness). Cupid's bow — the curve of the upper lip. Upper:lower ratio ≈1:1.6.
Philtrum
The groove between nose and upper lip; a short philtrum looks younger.

Skin & grooming

Skin quality
Texture, even tone, pores, absence of acne/scarring. The strongest softmax lever — skin improves fast with care.
Hairline / NW
The hairline; the Norwood scale (NW1–NW7) grades male hair loss.
Grooming
Haircut matched to face shape, brow shaping, beard/stubble, skincare. Huge gains at zero surgical cost.
Body fat
Losing excess fat “reveals” the cheekbones and jawline.
